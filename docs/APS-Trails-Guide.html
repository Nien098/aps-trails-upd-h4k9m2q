Walking the trail, step by step

Open the app

Look for the red mountain icon on your home screen — the one labeled APS Trails — and tap it.

The app already has its trail maps saved on your phone, so it still works with no internet and no phone signal — perfect for the woods.
1

Pick a trail to walk

You'll see a list of saved trails on the home screen. Tap the one you want — it'll open up so you can look at it before you head out.

  • Check the distance and estimated time so you know what you're in for.
  • Not sure which one? Ask whoever made the trail (like Dad) which one is right for today.
2

Start your walk

Once you're ready to go, look for the big green Start button and tap it. Your phone will begin tracking your walk with GPS — you'll see a blue line on the map showing exactly where you are on the trail.

Keep your phone somewhere with a clear view of the sky (like a pocket, not deep in a backpack) so GPS can find you easily.
3

Read your cue cards

As you walk, cards will pop up telling you what to do next — like "turn left" or "watch your step." Each card also shows a number, so you always know which stop you're on.

Once you've passed a cue, its marker turns grey on the map — that's your at-a-glance way to see what's done and what's still ahead.

4

Pause & Resume

Need a water break, or to tie a shoe? Tap Pause at the top of the screen. The clock stops, the map stops moving, and everything waits for you.

Ready to keep going? Tap Resume in that same spot — it'll be green while you're paused.

You can also pause or resume from the notification at the top of your phone, without even opening the app.
5

Skip a cue (if you need to)

Sometimes GPS can be a little off. If a cue card seems out of place, look for the small Skip ⏭ button next to it and tap it — it moves you on to the next one.

Only skip a cue if you're sure you've actually passed that spot. When in doubt, ask a grown-up.
6

If the app closes by itself

Don't worry — your walk isn't lost! Just reopen APS Trails. It will ask: "Resume unfinished walk?"

  • Tap Resume to pick up right where you left off — same spot, same time, same cues.
  • Tap Discard only if you actually want to start that walk over.
7

Finish your walk

Reached the end? Tap the red Stop button. Your walk gets saved automatically — distance, time, and all — so you can look back on it later.

A few extra trail signs

Grown-up feature

Making a trail

Someone can draw a brand-new trail and add their own cue cards, or even let the app auto-generate a route in an area. That's usually a job for whoever manages the trails, like Dad — there's a full advanced guide for that.

Good to know

Reverse direction

Any saved trail can be flipped to walk it backwards — handy for out-and-back routes. Look for Reverse direction in a trail's menu.

Housekeeping

Getting updates

Every so often the app will offer a new version. Tap to download, then tap Install and Open when Android asks — that's it.

Quick questions

Do I need internet or phone signal to use it?

Nope! Once a trail is saved on your phone, the map and directions all work offline. That's the whole point — it's built for places without signal.

What if I wander off the trail path?

That's okay — the app keeps tracking you with GPS no matter where you are. Just head back toward the line on the map, and the cue cards will pick back up.

Will pausing mess up my walk time?

No — paused time doesn't count. Your total walking time only adds up while you're actually moving, not while you're taking a break.

What do the grey cue markers mean?

Grey means "already done" — either you walked past it, or you skipped it on purpose. It's just there to help you keep track of where you are.

My phone battery is low — should I worry?

GPS tracking does use some battery. If you're heading out for a long walk, it's smart to start with a full charge or bring a portable charger.

Happy trails!

Getting a trail onto the phone

Two ways to start a trail

From the home screen, tap the New trail button and choose one of two starting points.

Draw on map

"Tap out the path and cues by hand" — build a route from your armchair, or on-site referencing the map.

  • Full manual control over every point and cue
  • Can optionally follow real mapped trails between taps
  • Best when you already know the route you want

Record by walking

"Walk the trail now — GPS traces the path and drops cues" — go for the actual walk and let the phone do the drawing.

  • Recording starts automatically once GPS locks on — there's no separate Start button, just a red Stop when you're done
  • Keeps the screen awake and tracks in the background the whole walk
  • On Stop, your GPS trace is automatically cleaned up and snapped onto the nearest mapped trail, then handed to you in the editor to name, check, and save

Either path lands you in the same trail editor afterward, so cues, colours, and saving all work identically from here regardless of which way you started.

The trail editor

The mode bar

A card pinned to the bottom of the map controls whether you're drawing path or placing cues.

Draw path / Add cue
A two-way switch in the middle of the bar. Everything you tap on the map behaves differently depending which side is selected — see Drawing a path and Placing cues.
Auto-generate icon
Leading icon on the bar — opens the generator sheet. See Auto-generating a trail.
Show more / Show less
Expands the bar to reveal an extra toggle (Follow trails in Draw mode, Free placement in Add cue mode) plus a live hint line — or collapses back down to just the mode switch and the running trail length.
✕ Hide
Shrinks the whole bar down to a small round button in the corner, out of the way when you need to see the full map. Tap that button again to bring it back exactly as you left it.
Trail length
Always visible at the bottom of the bar, updating live as you add points.
Draw path mode

Drawing a path

With the mode bar set to Draw path:

Tap the map
Adds a point. If Follow trails is on, the segment bends along real mapped trail geometry between this point and the last one; off, it's a straight line between them.
Tap an existing point
Routes the path through it — handy for retracing part of a loop.
Long-press a point
Opens a sheet: Move this point, or Delete this point (confirms "Delete this point? — Removes the point and re-joins the trail.").
Long-press the line
Inserts a brand-new point wherever you pressed, splitting that stretch in two.
Undo icon (app bar)
Removes the last point you added.
Mark here
Floating button that drops a point (or opens the cue editor, in Add cue mode) at your own live GPS position — useful when authoring on-site.
Compass / target FABs
Small buttons to reset the map to face north and flat, or to re-centre on your current location.
Add cue mode

Placing and editing cues

Switch the mode bar to Add cue to work with cue markers instead of the path.

Tap empty map
Opens the cue editor at that spot, ready to create a new cue.
Tap an existing cue
Reopens the same editor pre-filled, to change or delete it.
Tap a stacked cue
When two or more cues sit within a metre of each other, tapping shows "{N} cues stacked here" — a list to pick which one to edit, plus an option to Add another cue at this same spot.
Long-press a cue
Starts moving it — an amber banner reads 'Moving "{label}" — tap the map to place it'. The new spot snaps onto the drawn path by default, or drops exactly where you tap if Free placement is switched on.
The cue editor sheet

Setting up a single cue

Opens as New cue or Edit cue depending on context, with these fields top to bottom:

Position in list
New cues only. 1 = first; defaults to the end of the list. Type a smaller number to insert it earlier — everything after shifts up automatically.
Cue type
A row of type chips — see the full table below. Picking a type auto-fills the map label and spoken text with sensible defaults (only if you haven't already typed your own).
Map label (short)
The short text shown on the marker/card, e.g. "Turn left".
Spoken direction
What gets read aloud when the walker reaches this cue.
Trigger distance
A slider, 10–60 m (default 25 m) — how close the walker needs to be for the cue to fire.

Shortcut for new routes

Auto-generating a trail

Tap the auto-generate icon in the mode bar to build a route from whatever trails are visible on screen right now — pan/zoom to the area first.

Length
Slider from 500 m to 15,000 m, in ~250 m steps (default 4,000 m), with Short / Medium / Long reference labels below it.
Loop / There & back
Shape of the generated route. Loop is the default.
Add turn directions
Checkbox, on by default — automatically drops spoken "turn left / right" cues along the generated route.
Generate
Builds the route. If nothing's found nearby, it'll say so rather than guess — zoom into an area with mapped trails and try again.
Generating replaces the trail's current path (and cues, if "Add turn directions" is checked) — you'll be asked to confirm first if one already exists.

Trail colours

This trail's colour (in the editor)

The colour-swatch icon in the trail editor's app bar sets the colour of the trail you're actively drawing or editing — it always draws on top of everything else, in its own colour, so it's never ambiguous which one you're working on.

Home menu → Safety & battery settings

Safety & battery settings

If a walker stops moving for a while, this nudges them on-screen and out loud, then — if they don't respond — texts an emergency contact their last known location. Cell signal is required to send; it's a helpful nudge, not a substitute for calling for help.

Enable safety alert
Off by default. Turning it on will request SMS permission.
Send emergency SMS
On by default. Switch off to keep this a reminder-only nudge that never texts anyone — it just keeps repeating until acknowledged or the walker moves.
Emergency contact phone number
Saved as you type.
Nudge after being still for
2–60 minutes, default 8 — how long before the first nudge.
Then send the alert after another
Default 8 more minutes before the SMS actually goes out (or the reminder repeats, if SMS is off).
Send test message
Sends a real test SMS to the configured number, so you can confirm it actually works before relying on it.
Show stillness debug overlay
Testing only — overlays live phase/timing/GPS-accuracy info on the walk screen.

What actually happens on the phone

An ongoing notification appears with an "I'm OK" action (can't be swiped away), the phone vibrates, and it speaks aloud. If it escalates, the on-screen card turns red while the SMS sends, then green once it's confirmed sent. Tapping I'm OK — on the notification or the in-app card — cancels everything and resets.

Battery settings

Background tracking and the safety alert both need Android to not restrict this app's battery use, or they can stop working with the screen off. This section shows whether the app is currently unrestricted, with a one-tap Fix if not.

Samsung phones keep a separate power-saving list. Check Settings → Battery → Background usage limits (or Device care → Battery) and make sure APS Trails isn't in "Sleeping apps" or "Deep sleeping apps".

Backing up, restoring & importing

Back up my data
Creates a single backup file covering every trail and all walk history, ready to share/save wherever you like (email, Drive, a USB cable, etc.).
Restore from a backup
Open the saved .tgbackup file from your Files app, Drive, or an email, and choose APS Trails to open it with — it merges everything back in without deleting anything already on the phone. Duplicates are automatically skipped.

The app also recognizes a few other files opened with it directly: a shared trail file from another install (imports straight in), or a GPX / Runkeeper cardioActivities.csv export (merged into walk history).

Keeping the app updated

The app checks quietly every time it opens, and downloads automatically over Wi‑Fi — but per its own design, it never installs anything without a tap from you.

Home screen banner
Appears once something's available/downloading/ready, e.g. "Update available — tap to download". Tap it to open the full Update screen.
Download update
Requires Wi‑Fi — downloads are deliberately never done over mobile data.
Allow installs
Only shown the first time — deep-links to the Android permission screen for installing this app's own updates.
Install update
Hands the file to Android's own installer. Android will always ask you to confirm the install itself — that final tap is normal and can't be skipped.

Walk history & tracker details

Every walk is saved automatically, and there are three linked places to review it — overall, per-trail, and per-walk.

Overall History screen

All time header
Total walks, total distance, and total elevation gained across every trail ever walked.
Last 7 days
Rolling weekly walk count and distance, right below the all-time header.
Calories use weight
Tap this line to set body weight (kg or lb, matching your units setting) — used only to estimate calories burned; nothing else in the app depends on it.
Walk list
Every walk ever recorded, newest first — trail name, date, distance, time, pace, and elevation gain at a glance. Tap one to open its full detail.
Trends & records icon
Top-right of the History screen — opens longer-term trend charts and personal records across all trails.

Per-trail Progress screen

Opened from a trail's ⋮ → Progress menu item — only every walk on that one trail, so you can see whether a specific route is getting easier over time.

Best pace / best time
Your fastest walk on this trail, marked with a 🏆 trophy icon further down in the list.
Trend note
Once there are 2+ walks: "X% faster/slower than your first walk here" — a plain-language read on whether you're improving on this specific route.
Time per walk chart
A bar per walk, shortest bar = fastest — an at-a-glance shape of your history on this trail.
Every walk
Full list for this trail only, newest first, each showing distance/time/pace. Tap any entry for its full detail.

Single-walk detail

Opened by tapping any walk from either screen above (also the summary screen shown right after finishing a walk).

Stat grid
Distance, time, average pace, average speed, elevation gained, estimated calories, moving time, and moving pace (moving time/pace exclude long standing-still stretches, e.g. a paused break).
Route map
The actual path walked, drawn on the map from the recorded GPS track.
Your progress on this trail
Jumps straight to that trail's Progress screen above.
Elevation & pace charts
Two graphs plotted against distance along the walk — elevation profile (lightly smoothed) and pace — shown whenever there's enough GPS track data.
Splits
Time for each completed km/mile (matching your units setting), plus any trailing partial distance.
Delete
Top-right icon (not shown on the just-finished summary screen) — confirms "Delete this walk? Removes it from your history. Trail totals stay." Only removes this one walk record; the trail itself is untouched.
Deleting a trail (from the home list) does not delete its walk history — those records stay in the overall History screen, just no longer linked to a "Progress" shortcut from a trail row that no longer exists.
